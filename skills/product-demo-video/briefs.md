# Brief templates

Fill `[BRACKETS]` from the intake answers and the captured assets, save the result as
`docs/brief.md`, then execute it yourself. Do not hand these to the user to run.

Start every brief with one line: who watches, what they believe before the film, and what they
should believe or do after it. Cut any shot that doesn't serve that change.

Then, before any template, write the **story device** (research.md, "What makes it look made by a
designer"). A brief without it is a feature list, and a feature list renders as slop:

```
Logline:  [one sentence: the viewer's problem, shown not said, and how the product ends it]
Motif:    [one object from the product or the problem that opens the film: a to-do list, an
           empty inbox, a checkbox, the product's own icon] and how it comes back changed.
Carrier:  [the object that becomes every next scene: a dot, a ticket, one number, the logo's
           parts] and its chain, e.g. dot → pill → card → phone → icon. Plus the product's own
           idea as a transition (markdown → rendered text, receipt → booked row), if it has one.
World:    [the set: light pools that change colour per chapter / a desk under window light /
           night sky / the product's own background] and what changes in it over the film.
Hero UI:  [the 3-5 real UI moments that prove it, each named by asset file; for each, what the
           product does by itself (types, inserts, reorders, fills, counts)]
Pace:     [where it rushes, where it breathes (1-1.8 s reading holds), where the drop lands]
Payoff:   [the last line or image, which answers the first frame]
```

## I. Device chapter walkthrough (default for walkthroughs)

From Amanda Sartini's planner walkthrough (research.md). Use it when the product is best shown as
it is, in a phone and/or a Mac, one feature per chapter. Calm, premium, readable.

```
<direction>
A [DURATION]-second walkthrough of [PRODUCT], one continuous take on one set: [BRAND BG]
lit by one large soft pool of light (ambient) behind the device. Each chapter has its own light
colour ([COLOUR 1] → [COLOUR 2] → …); one chapter may turn the whole set dark ([DARK CHAPTER]).
Left: chapterBlock with a word kicker (no numbers), a light first line and a bold second line,
one muted sentence. Right: the real [phone / laptop] (assets/…), rising once from below on a
heavy spring, then never leaving. The camera pushes into the device until it crops at the frame
edge, on exactly the part each chapter shows, and glides to the next part between chapters.
The product performs by itself: typed input, inserted and reordered cards (stackY), tab slides,
typing dots, live counters. A callout (hairline + label) names what just happened. No cursor
unless a person would really click, no rings, no HUD labels.
Reading holds of 1.2-1.8 s are fine while the device floats (idleFloat) and the light drifts.
Banned: crossfades, whole-frame blur-ins, glows, particles, numbered chapters, flat voids.
</direction>

<structure>
[BPM] BPM. Hook (0-2 s): "[LINE A]." rises, then "[LINE B]." in bold as the device rises into frame.
Chapter per feature, [4-6 s] each: the previous block dims to grey (0.3 s) and leaves; the new
kicker "[WORD]" and lines "[LIGHT LINE] / [BOLD LINE]" rise; the light pool changes colour;
the camera glides to [UI REGION]; the app does [REAL ACTION]; 1-2 callouts "[WHAT IT DID]".
Ending: the [second device] slides in beside the first; one line: "[PAYOFF LINE]". Wordmark or
URL small under it. Hold about 2.5 s with the devices floating.
</structure>
```

## J. Story film (motif, character or world)

From Lex Lin's Boon film (research.md). Use it for launches where the feeling matters as much as
the features: assistants, consumer apps, anything with a mascot or a strong idea.

```
<direction>
A [DURATION]-second story film for [PRODUCT], one continuous take. Logline: [LOGLINE].
The motif [MOTIF] opens the film in close-up, out of focus, pulled sharp as the camera pulls
back to show the problem at scale ([PROBLEM IMAGE]). The product enters as [CHARACTER / ITS
ICON / ITS UI], asks or offers in a short line whose words pull into focus (focusIn), and
becomes the transition itself (grows to fill the frame, its shape opens the next world).
Real surfaces: [the phone on a desk under window light (gobo) / the lock screen / the app]. Time
passes through light (day → dusk → night) and real UI (a clock, a notification).
The user's request turns into the result: its key words highlight, lift off and fly into the
result's fields (highlight + flyTag). Chapters are verbs ("[VERB 1]." "[VERB 2]." …), each a
colour field with the problem blurred behind and one real result card; whip pans between them
on the beat. The ending returns to the motif: [HOW IT RESOLVES], then the payoff "[LINE]".
Banned: crossfades, whole-frame blur-ins, glows, decorative particles, numbered chapters.
</direction>

<structure>
[BPM] BPM. Problem (0-4 s) · offer (4-7 s) · the product at work on a real surface (7-17 s) ·
time passes (17-21 s) · verb chapters, one per [2-3 s] (21-33 s) · the motif resolves (33-37 s) ·
payoff and logo (37-40 s), last frame answers the first.
</structure>
```

## K. Object chain launch (one carrier through the whole film)

From the Prompt Motion library (research.md): ViralGrow, Taxtello, BlueVisa, Dub. Use it for a
15-25 s launch where the product has one strong object (a number, a document, a card, the logo).

```
<direction>
A [DURATION]-second launch film for [PRODUCT], one continuous take on one world with one camera.
One object carries the film: [CARRIER], which becomes every next scene ([CHAIN, e.g. dot → pill →
card → phone → app icon]). About four visual ideas; three must be good enough to use as still ads.
Arc: [CHAOS] → [CONTROL] → [CLARITY] → [BRAND]. The product's own idea is the key transition:
[e.g. the receipt flies into its booking row; the long URL squeezes into the short link].
Every shot enters already moving (arrive) and leaves accelerating (depart); the camera drifts
1.0 → 1.04 during holds and the next scene starts at the zoom the last one ended on. Never two
camera moves at once. Data is continuous: [HERO NUMBER] counts on an odometer, survives the scene
change and lands in the UI that owns it. A connector or flyTag draws every cause → effect.
[BRAND BG] world lit by ambient pools, [ACCENT] rationed to the active state, [DISPLAY FONT] for
one-line headlines (at most 4 words), mono for numbers.
Banned: crossfades, dips to black, the same layout twice in a row, micro labels nobody can read,
generic globes/vortexes, AI faces or stock photos, highlight boxes behind words, dead end cards.
</direction>

<structure>
[BPM] BPM. 0-1.5 s the carrier appears and already does something premium (a glint draws the
mark, a dot pops with a halo). Then one idea per [3-4] s, each grown out of the last:
[IDEA 1 → IDEA 2 → IDEA 3 → IDEA 4]. One burst before the payoff (a 16th-note run or 0.25 s
cards), then rest. Ending: the scene collapses into the app icon or the logo's letter, the
wordmark opens from it (revealFrom), one line, one CTA; about 1.7 s of stillness with a drift.
</structure>
```

## L. Keynote sting (10-15 s, cuts at peak blur)

From notdwd's frame-for-frame remake of a keynote launch sting (research.md). Fast, glassy,
square or 16:9, for a feature drop or a social teaser.

```
<direction>
A [DURATION]-second product sting for [PRODUCT], [FORMAT]. Apple-keynote feel: soft, bright, glassy.
No shot starts or ends on a still frame: each enters already moving on a hard expo ease-out
(arrive, most of the distance in the first frames) and leaves on an accelerating move or a blur
ramp. Hard cuts only on the music, about 2 frames before the beat, at peak blur (cutBlur), and
each lands on a match (the same mark in a new context). Blur follows speed and direction.
A cursor with intent: it glides in, turns toward where it's going, grows on hover, shrinks on press.
Glass panels spring out of their anchor with one soft overshoot (about 2.5%) and a refracting edge.
Banned: crossfades, frozen frames (except one deliberate hold), stock UI kits, glows on UI text.
</direction>

<structure>
Write it as a keyframe table per shot (frame numbers at 30 fps, values per frame for anything
that must feel measured), e.g. S1 f0-71 header rises y932 → 776 → 683 → 608 → 546 → 536, then
drifts 1 px/frame. Typical chain: header in a light window → cursor clicks the logo → cut on the
drop to the app icon with satellites orbiting into place (orbit) → it collapses into a menu bar →
a glass prompt springs out and types → whip-tilt to the answer, which arrives smeared and settles
(arrive + cutBlur) → a card rises over it → stacked cards with frosted glass titles (glassText) →
the logo disc holds → cut to the wordmark arriving huge and streaking (zoomSmear), settling.
</structure>
```

## E. Keynote continuous take (default look)

Adapted from zero's launch-film prompt (see research.md). The template `index.html` is a
working 11 s version of this structure; extend it, don't start over.

```
<direction>
An Apple-keynote launch film for [PRODUCT] ([URL]), one continuous take, [DURATION] seconds.
Every scene is made out of the previous one: nothing fades, blurs in or cuts. Objects change
shape instead: text rises out of a mask line, cards pop from zero on a spring, numbers count up,
a black shape floods the whole frame and contracts into the next scene.
[BRAND BG] canvas, [BRAND FG] UI, one accent [ACCENT] used for the dot and the period.
[DISPLAY FONT] 800 with tight tracking for headlines and the wordmark, [UI FONT] for UI,
mono for numbers and kickers. A cursor drives every change with real clicks. The camera zooms
screen-studio style toward whatever the cursor touches, and the cursor scales with it.
The real product UI sits on a 3D plane that straightens as the camera pushes in; the UI behind
goes soft while the focus card stays sharp, and the UI bleeds off the frame edge.
Banned: crossfades, blur-ins, brightness "developing", 3D flips, particles, glows, holds longer
than 1 s, anything that looks like a template.
</direction>

<structure>
[BPM] BPM, [BEATS] beats, something happens on every beat.
Open: the accent dot grows into a black pill, "[CTA VERB]" rises inside it, the cursor clicks,
and the pill floods the frame.
Product: the flood contracts onto [REAL SCREEN] tilted in 3D with a two-line headline
"[LINE 1]. / [LINE 2]." (accent periods). The headline leaves through its mask line as the
camera pushes into [FEATURE 1].
Features: [FEATURE 1], [FEATURE 2], [FEATURE 3], each a real cursor action on a sharp focus
card over the blurred UI, each result landing on the next beat (toast, toggle, chart draws).
Proof: [METRIC] counts up on a card attached to the UI.
End: the scene collapses into the accent dot, which travels to become the period of the
[PRODUCT] wordmark. CTA pill and URL typed under it. Hold about 2.5 s.
[IF LOOPING: the last frame equals the first.]
</structure>

<build>
Use lib/kit.js: maskRise, scramble, typeOn, countUp, pill, card, chip, toggle, cursorPath,
camera, plane3d, blurred, morphBox, flood, contract, iris, wordmark. Scenes take absolute t,
events on B(n). Every visible event gets an SFX in sfx_cues.json. Render stills with
--stills beats, run check.sh, and fix every hold and pop the motion audit reports.
</build>
```

## F. Agent / chat product (ask → think → drop → result → act)

Adapted from ultima's food-craving film (research.md). Use it for any product where the user
types a request and the product does the work: assistants, agents, search, copilots.

```
<direction>
A [DURATION]-second launch film for [PRODUCT], one continuous take. Each scene grows from the
last: text rises from a mask line, buttons expand into pages, color floods from one object
before shrinking into another. The camera makes one glide per scene (camPath, log-space zoom)
and never zooms in and out back to back or reverses mid-move.
[BRAND BG] canvas, [BRAND FG] UI, accent [ACCENT]. Real [RESULT TYPE] from ./assets on the
result cards. [UI FONT] for UI, [DISPLAY FONT] for the end card.
Banned: crossfades, blur-ins, 3D flips, particles, glows, reversing camera, holds over 1 s.
</direction>

<structure>
[BPM] BPM, [BEATS] beats. Start the song [N] beats before its drop.
Ask (beats 0-6): push slowly into the real composer, about 3×. Type "[THE USER'S REAL REQUEST]"
on fixed lines (typeOn with \n) with a natural keystroke rhythm, one key sound per letter.
Send (beat 6): press send; an accent ripple turns the message into a chat bubble. Pull back
once to reveal the product around it.
Think (beats 6-12): the send button becomes [THINKING OBJECT from the product], which does
something on every beat under the product's real working label "[LABEL]".
Drop (beat 12, on the song's drop): an accent flood from the thinking object clears the
farthest corner in about 0.35 s; [RESULT CARDS] burst in; the strip slides and lands on
"[BEST RESULT]", which lifts with a "[BADGE]" pill.
Result: the flood contracts back into the bubble, carrying its words, while the chosen card
flies into its slot. The product's reply rises in: "[REAL REPLY]".
Act: two pills, "[PRIMARY ACTION]" and "[SECONDARY]". The cursor clicks the primary →
spinner → "✓ [DONE STATE]".
Outro: the button expands into the page, staying pill-shaped as it reaches the edges, while the
camera pushes into it. Its label grows to headline size and slides away. "[LINE 1]" / "[LINE 2]"
word by word. The accent dot travels and becomes the period of the wordmark.
</structure>

<gotchas>
Every handoff is a shared element (the flood carries the bubble's words, the button carries its
label). Draw order is layer order: cards never float above the flood. Keep typed text on fixed
lines. Declare every variable before the first seek().
</gotchas>
```

## G. Inside the period (looping, square or 16:9)

Adapted from zero's nightshift template (research.md). The whole film happens inside the
wordmark's period; it suits products that work while the user is away (automation, agents,
monitoring, scheduling).

```
<direction>
A [DURATION]-second looping launch film. Outside the period: [OUTSIDE WORLD, e.g. the product's
real site or a daytime photo] behind a black "[product]." wordmark. Inside the period: [INSIDE
WORLD] under glass panels (glass(): blurred backdrop, tint, inner edge, rim). Nothing cuts:
shapes morph, dots fly, colors change under floods. Every morph glides 0.8 s on
cubic-bezier(.45,0,.15,1) (glide / keyed / states), and the camera rides the same curve.
Banned: flat or gradient backgrounds made in code, camera zooms into tiny points, hard cuts,
crossfades, bouncy springs, particles, glows, holds over 1 s, cropped type.
</direction>

<structure>
Open: the letters squeeze into the period (squeeze, at: 'dot') while it glides to the middle.
The period opens like an iris (or portal) onto the inside world; "[HOOK LINE 1]" then
"[HOOK LINE 2]" roll through one mask line.
Ask: a glass "Ask [product]" pill springs in, the cursor clicks it, it glides into an input
and "[THE ONE JOB]" types out.
Work: send shrinks the input to a dot that ripples into [A GRID / LIST of the real objects];
a scan sweeps it and a counter counts [REAL NUMBER] (countUp reads frame time).
Results: the working shape turns black and goo-splits (goo) into [3 REAL RESULTS] pills;
checks pop on beats.
Proof: [REAL SCREEN from ./assets] with one hover and one number that counts.
Out: a flood from the proof becomes "[CTA] →", the cursor clicks, the button shrinks into a
dot, the scene closes into it like an iris, and the letters slide back out of the period.
Last frame = first frame.
</structure>

<build>
One morph table (states()) of beat times for the shape that carries the film, so retiming is
one edit. Render the inside world with layerCtx/withCtx and reveal it through the period with
portal() or iris(); never zoom the camera into the dot. Ease the closing iris by area.
</build>
```

## H. Explainer (how it works, one idea)

Adapted from Meaghan Choi's motion prompt sheet. Use it when the viewer has to understand a
mechanism (how the product does X), not just see that it exists. Slower than the launch
templates: one idea at a time, voice on.

```
<direction>
A [DURATION]-second animated explainer of [THE ONE IDEA, e.g. how PRODUCT does X] for
[AUDIENCE]. The question it answers, in the viewer's words: "[QUESTION]". They start believing
[THE FAMILIAR WRONG PICTURE] and leave understanding [THE CORRECT MODEL].
One continuous take on [BRAND BG]: objects carry across scenes (a dot becomes a node, a line
becomes a path, a card becomes a row). Every movement shows grouping, order, cause or scale;
nothing moves for decoration. One focal action per scene, then a hold of up to 2 s so the key
frame can be read. The picture shows the mechanism; the voice says why it matters.
Real product UI and real numbers from ./assets only, each with its source in assets/README.md.
Banned: crossfades, blur-ins, glows, particles, decorative camera moves, perpetual drifting,
generic AI imagery, more than 8 words per line on screen.
</direction>

<structure>
0-10%    the familiar wrong picture: [HOW PEOPLE DO IT TODAY], shown, not described.
10-35%   the smallest correct model: [2-4 ELEMENTS AND HOW THEY CONNECT], built out of the
         wrong picture's own pieces.
35-75%   one concrete example: [A REAL CASE IN THE PRODUCT], step by step on the real UI.
75-90%   change one variable: [WHAT CHANGES], and show the effect ([REAL NUMBER] counts up).
90-100%  return to the opening image, now understood; wordmark and [CTA].
</structure>

<build>
In docs/shotlist.md, every shot also names the one thing the viewer learns and how it reads
with the sound off. Voiceover on (append C); captions with captions.mjs. SFX only on the changes
the viewer must notice, and one recurring musical motif. Audit with ./check.sh ... --max-hold 2.
</build>
```

## A. Kinetic launch

```
Make a dynamic [DURATION]-second launch film for [PRODUCT] ([URL]), with the energy of a
motion designer's showreel. Go all out.

Assets
- Use real screenshots (Playwright), the real logo, real colors and fonts from ./assets.
- Never redraw the product UI from imagination. Crop and animate the real thing.

Story (a change on every beat, a new idea every bar)
1. Hook: [PROBLEM] in 5 words or fewer, slammed word by word on full color fields (slam()).
2. Optional numbered chapter labels ("01 / CONNECT") in mono, scrambling in and out.
3. The product appears; the UI assembles itself piece by piece on a plane3d tilt.
4. [FEATURE 1], [FEATURE 2], [FEATURE 3]: each a UI moment with a cursor doing a real action.
5. The old way struck through (strike()), then one number that proves it: [METRIC] counting up.
6. Wordmark with the accent period + [CTA].

Hard cuts are allowed here, but only on downbeats, and every cut is covered by a sound.
Sound: [AUDIO PLAN]. A real SFX on every event, placed by its peak.
Format: [PRIMARY FORMAT] first, then [OTHER FORMATS] from the same timeline, reframed not cropped.
```

## B. UI morph loop (SaaS / app UIs)

```
<direction>
Product-film UI motion. One container never cuts: every state is the same element changing
size, radius and fill (morphBox) while its content rises in and out of its own mask (maskRise).
A cursor drives every change.
[BRAND BG] canvas, one accent [ACCENT]. Springs with at most a tiny overshoot.
Banned: bouncy easing, glows, gradients on UI chrome, particle bursts, dead time.
</direction>

<structure>
[BPM] BPM, [BARS] bars, something happens on every beat.
[8-12 UI STATES FROM THE REAL PRODUCT, e.g. logo -> CTA button -> email field (typed) -> loader
-> success check -> dashboard card -> chart draws itself -> tooltip -> Cmd-K palette -> toast -> logo]
Each state shows the real data from the product screenshot it came from.
</structure>

<build>
1. window.seek(t) only. No CSS transitions, timers, or carried state.
2. A value with many targets = track() (one spring per change).
3. Text inside a morphing container uses swapAlpha(): in after the morph starts, out before the next.
4. Tab indicators use indicator() so leading and trailing edges stretch.
5. Start on a downbeat. UI sounds on measured hits.
</build>

<gotchas>
The last frame must equal the first, cursor position and velocity included (loopT).
</gotchas>
```

## C. Voiceover / mascot add-on (append to any template)

```
Voice: write a [DURATION]-second script (about 2.3 words per second) in [LANGUAGE].
Write it as vo_script.json, one line per shot: [{"t": <shot start>, "text": "..."}].
Generate it with `node voiceover.mjs say --provider [PROVIDER] --voice [VOICE_ID]`
(key read from .env by name). Fix every overrun it reports, then time each on-screen beat
to the phrase it illustrates. Mix with `./mix.sh ... --voice audio/voice.wav` (ducks music),
then `node captions.mjs --mux out/final_<format>.mp4` for each format.
[Optional] Mascot: [DESCRIPTION, or the kit's box character]. Draw it with mascot(): fixed
proportions and palette, squash and stretch when it lands, seeded blinks, eyes that follow the
cursor (look). It reacts on downbeats. Identity never changes between shots.
```

Never paste a real key into a prompt or file other than `.env`.

## D. Director's brief (45 s+, flagship launches)

```
You are the director, animator, sound designer and render engineer for a [DURATION] product
film made in code. Treat this as a multi-session production. Don't rush to a final render.

## The film in one line
[LOGLINE. What the viewer should feel and do at the end.]

## References and inputs
- ./refs/ : [reference]. Take the grammar, never the content.
- ./assets/ : real product captures. ./audio/ : [track or synthesized score].

## Look
[3-5 lines: palette, type, texture, camera language. Banned looks.]

## Beat sheet
0:00-0:02  hook: [the single most striking image]
0:02-...   [acts] - a new visual payoff every 3-5 seconds
[END]      logo + [CTA]; last frame sets up the first if it loops

## Workflow, with gates
1. docs/style_guide.md and docs/shotlist.md (every shot: time, camera, text, SFX).
2. Stills for every shot -> contact sheet -> critique.
3. Animatic at 960x540 (render.mjs --w 960 --h 540 --sub 1). Fix pacing before polish.
4. Full animation, polish pass, sound pass, final render.
5. For 4+ chapters, write docs/ANIMATION_GUIDE.md first, then split chapters across
   subagents so every subagent codes in the same style.
```

## Reference extraction (when the user gives a video, X post, frame or folder)

```
1. Video or post: node ref.mjs <x.com status URL | .mp4 URL | file> [--name <name>].
   Open refs/<name>/sheet.png and read refs/<name>/analysis.md (cuts, shot lengths, palette,
   LUFS, motion per second, holds). If the post text contains the creator's prompt, study its
   direction and structure. Frame/folder: use as-is.
2. Write docs/style_guide.md: palette (hex), type (family feel, weight, tracking, case), how
   text enters and leaves, transition types, camera moves, UI treatment (flat, 3D tilt, depth
   blur), pacing (beats per change, longest hold), sound feel.
3. Map each transition to a kit function (research.md, last table). Build anything missing
   in lib/kit.js as a pure function of t.
4. Take the grammar of the reference, never its content, logos or characters.
```

## Critique pass (run after every stills or full render)

```
Open the contact sheet (and strip.png / phone.png after a full render) and look at them
properly. Be a harsh motion director, not a proud author.

Score 1-10: hook in first 2s · readability at phone size · motion quality (springs, no dead
frames) · continuity (each scene made out of the last) · variety (a change every beat) ·
composition · depth (camera, plane3d tilt, focus card over soft UI, shadows that grow with
height) · story (a motif that returns, a payoff, a world that changes) · craft (would a motion
designer sign it: no slop tells, see below) · polish (no ghosts, no smears, no cropped type) · brand accuracy · sound sync ·
accessibility (contrast 4.5:1, at most 8 words per line, nothing said by colour alone, the
story follows with the sound off).

After a full render, read the motion audit in out/check/motion.txt first: every hold over 1 s
(2 s for explainers), every single-frame pop that isn't a deliberate downbeat cut, every flash
warning, every silent visible event and every blind sound is an automatic problem.

List the 3 biggest problems with timestamps. Hunt for: a crossfade or blur-in where a shape
should transform, a flood that doesn't clear the corners, text overlapping during swaps,
descenders peeking under a mask line, anything sliding instead of springing, a small centered
screenshot instead of UI that fills and bleeds off the frame, a camera that doesn't move toward
the action, corner labels and frame borders, blurry scaled text, a dead beat, a stutter at the
loop seam, invented UI that is not in ./assets, a camera that reverses or zooms in then out,
a zoom into a tiny dot, smeared counters or typed text, ghost copies on fast moves (open the
fastest frame at 1:1), cards above a flood, type cropped by the frame, typed text that wraps,
a handoff that carries nothing across, a sound with nothing moving or a move with no sound,
pale text on a light card or over busy UI, a line of more than 8 words, a state shown only by a
colour change, a beat that only makes sense if you hear the voice.

Then the slop check. Answer each with yes or no; every "yes" is a problem to fix this round:
- Could this film be about any other product if you swapped the logo?
- Is there no motif, or does the last shot not answer the first?
- Is any chapter labelled with a number or HUD ("01 / RESEARCH")? (Only template A, a
  kinetic showreel, may use them, and then as its whole look.)
- Is a full desktop page shown so small its text can't be read on a phone?
- Is any background a flat void (plain black or white with nothing lit)?
- Does a cursor, ring or chip appear on more than half the scenes?
- Do all chapters have the same shape (headline + screenshot + ring)?
- Is every beat equally busy, with no moment that breathes?
- Is the proof a bare number instead of the product showing the result?
- Is the end card logo + pill button with no payoff line, or does the film fizzle out or hold
  dead for 2.5 s+?
- Does any scene arrive or leave on a still frame, or does the camera reset its zoom at a handoff?
- Is there a crossfade double exposure or a dip to black anywhere?
- Is the same layout (headline left, visual right) used three or more times in a row?
- Is there a highlight box behind a word, a 0.05 s fade, or the same blur-in on every line?
- Does any transition not carry an object across (could you swap it for a generic wipe)?
- Are there AI faces, stock photos, placeholder bars, generic globes or vortexes?
If there's a reference, put its sheet.png next to your contact sheet and name the 3 places it
looks more expensive. Fix those first.

Fix them, re-render the affected stills, append scores + fixes to docs/review_log.md.

After the full render, if you can start a separate agent or subagent, have a fresh one that didn't
build the film score out/check/contact.png and the motion strips of the final MP4 with this same
pass (give it research.md and the brief, not your opinion of the film). Fix what it finds.
```
